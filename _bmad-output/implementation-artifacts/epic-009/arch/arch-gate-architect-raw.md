BLOCKER: Runner isolation has no implementable routing contract. Story 9.1 requires verification and publication to use separate registrations under the same `ubuntu-24.04` label, and registration scope is the only selector named. Nothing in the bounded artifacts explains how a Gitea scheduler distinguishes the protected publication job from an untrusted job in the same repository, while the negative test requires the protected registration to be unable to claim that untrusted job. The story also forbids label divergence inside the story, so every candidate tuple may be rejected without any specified passing topology. Define the concrete Gitea routing primitive, the trust-class selector jobs express, who may request it, and the negative proof that an untrusted workflow cannot reach the protected runner. If the answer needs labels or a topology exception, make the ADR and revise the story before execution.

BLOCKER: The story dependency is cyclic. Story 9.2 depends on Story 9.1, but Story 9.1's Definition of Done requires the observed transitive action closure for every certified scenario. Those certification scenarios and their evidence are owned by Story 9.2. Story 9.1 therefore cannot be done without executing its successor, and Story 9.2 cannot start until Story 9.1 is done. Limit Story 9.1 to the closure exercised by its baseline and structural preflight probes; put the full scenario closure and drift gate in Story 9.2, or merge the stories and remove the dependency.

BLOCKER: The certification gate verifies report shape, not evidence authenticity. `REQUIRED_GITEA_CASES` can prove that a Markdown row, URL, and conclusion exist, but the stories do not require those values to be acquired from Gitea or cryptographically/content-addressably bound to the pinned tuple, repository, source SHA, workflow revision, run attempt, job conclusions, and retained artifacts. A stale or fabricated report can therefore pass the declared mechanical gate and authorize the repository-owner migration. Define a machine-verifiable evidence boundary: capture immutable Gitea run/job metadata and artifact identities, retain raw exports or signed/hashed evidence, and verify that the human report is a projection of that evidence.

MAJOR: TLS trust and network authorization are conflated. A CA store trusts issuers; it does not allow or deny individual endpoints. Story 9.1 asks for an endpoint set derived from executable definitions and then proposes omitting an endpoint as a trust negative case, while also requiring minimal network access without naming an egress enforcement owner. Split the contracts: bootstrap and job trust stores need issuer inputs, rotation, and wrong/missing-issuer tests; egress policy needs host/port inputs, an enforcing firewall/proxy boundary, and denied-destination tests. State how public endpoints are trusted when private-CA interception is absent or present.

MAJOR: The credential architecture is incomplete for a least-privilege certification. The stories name a forge package token and generic job-local credentials, but not the complete per-job identities, secret inputs, minimum scopes, ownership boundaries, or credentials used for image publication, forge Release creation, and Git-alias mutation. They also lack cross-destination denial proofs. Add a credential matrix covering principal, secret/context name, granted scopes, protected owner/environment, permitted mutations, and forbidden mutations. Require distinct principals where blast radii differ, or record an explicit architecture trade-off if the pinned Gitea cannot provide them.

MAJOR: Failed-jobs-only recovery is not reproducible because no deterministic fault-injection seam or cleanup contract is specified. Revoking a credential, blocking a network route, or breaking a destination all create different failure timing and residual state. Define the staging-only failure mechanism and exact injection point after a known immutable mutation; specify pre-state, expected partial state, rerun operation, preservation checks, and cleanup postconditions for packages, images, releases, aliases, tokens, artifacts, and runner workspaces.

MAJOR: The forge-coordinate contract is referenced but not self-contained in the bounded implementation package. The stories say all coordinates derive from two context fields and demand unknown-forge and malformed-registry tests, but do not define accepted forge identities, normalization, port/path behavior, canonical image/package outputs, same-authority rules, or a failure taxonomy. The spec index exposes only headings, and the stories provide no resolvable Spec pointer, so spec alignment cannot supply the missing contract without widening scope. Add a resolvable specification reference or include the authoritative classifier/projection contract and table-driven cases in the story package.

MAJOR: The action-governance acceptance rule is weaker than the risk it governs. Recording an observed transitive closure and its resolution source does not require that an action is maintained, license-compatible, GA, on a supported major, or pinned according to whether it receives credentials. The fallback alone mentions owner policy and SHA pinning. Require the tuple record to include each action reference and resolved commit, publisher/trust class, maintenance/license/GA status, and supported major; require reviewed full SHAs for credential-bearing or unapproved third-party actions and an ADR plus exit condition for any exception.

MAJOR: The plan can authorize execution despite contradicting its own state. It reports `readiness: green`, no gaps, `stories_elaborated: 0`, and `arch_gate_summary.ran: false`, even though two elaborated stories and an architecture-gate input are present and this review finds blockers. Set readiness to blocked/red, correct the elaboration count, and populate the gate summary before any executor consumes the plan.

MAJOR: The release-action fallback crosses story boundaries ambiguously. Story 9.1 owns probing `git-action-release@v2`, says structural incompatibility rejects the tuple before Story 9.2, but also permits Story 9.2 to take a `softprops/action-gh-release` branch after a separate ADR. The bounded artifacts do not define whether Story 9.1 can close with a failed primary probe, who authors and approves the ADR, or whether the alternate action must return to Story 9.1's tuple preflight before certification. Make fallback selection a predecessor decision: either the primary path passes, or an approved alternate is incorporated into the pinned tuple and all Story 9.1 probes pass before Story 9.2 begins.
